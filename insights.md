# RETAIL ANALYTICS AND MANAGEMENT INSIGHTS

** SCOPE ** Insghts are based on completed orders running between January 2025 tp January 2026. Revenue is net of revenue and profit is defined as revenue minus the cost (quantity*cost_price). Total revenue is approximately sh.28.16 millions and profit is equal to sh.2.89m. This gives a profit margin of 12.0%

# Top categories and best way to increase profits

1. ** Findings**  - Moblie phones rank at the top with net revenue of sh.6.9million which is 28% of the net revenue, while Accessories are ranked last with sh.1.05million in revenue contributing to 4.5% of the total revenue. Although this is the case, Mobile Phones have a 10.5% profit margin compared to 42.4% from accesories.

2. ** Observation** - Every sh.1 million of accessories sold earns sh.424million in profit while the same value of mobile phones sold earns about sh. 105,000. This shows the lowest cost way to make profit is through accessories.

3. ** Reccomendation** - To expand the margin on other categories such as Mobile phones, Electronics and Home appliances, bundle the accessories like, chargers, earbuds, mouse etc and show them at checkout. Offer discounts on the big items through the accessories rather than the headline product e.g free charger.

# Customer Segments performance

1. **Findings** - VIP customers are 79% more than the Ratail customers and 48% more than corporate. VIP spend and an average of ksh.253,252, corporate ksh.170,654, and Retail ksh. 141,124 consecutively. 18 registered customers - 4 from Corporate and 14 from Ratail, never made purchases.

2. **Why it matters**: - This spending prediction is made from the segment label and can be leveraged to target offers and service. Retail is the volume engine, accounting for around 60% of revenue (Ksh 14.4 million) and 68% of the customer base. VIP makes up 13% of the customer base, but, approximately, 20% of the revenue. The 18 non-buyers are registered customers that have never been converted.

3. **Recommendation**: - Provide loyalty rewards and early access to VIP patrons so that they stay. Encourage Corporate customers to use bulk/multi item offers to push them into becoming VIP customers. Offer a first-purchase offer to the 18 non-buyers and monitor the number of Retail customers who are converted to VIP.

# Growth and Seasonality

1. **Finding:** December 2025 revenue was KSh 2,027,121, then January 2026 fell 84.1% to KSh 322,433, the lowest month in the dataset and 72% below the 21-month average of about KSh 1.15 million. The early months of 2025 were also weak (January KSh 434,903, February KSh 369,566, a 15.0% drop). In the other direction, September 2026 reached KSh 2,092,445, up 116% on September 2025 (KSh 968,118).

2. **Signifinace** - Growth is seasonal: the year end is a good year, and the year begins with a low. The year-on-year increase indicates growth in the business and doesn't infer that it is a slowdown.

3. **Recommendation**: Organize a promotion in January and February (not big discounts, accessory bundles, loyalty offers). Avoid selling stock, and/or reduce cash sales when prices are low; and/or increase stock sales when prices are high before year-end.

# customers drifts

1. **Obsevation** - There are 47 customers that have not received a completed order for more than 90 days prior to the last order date in the data (30 September 2026). The most recent of them (customers 44, 24, 102, 145 and 143) last bought in late June 2026, only 94 to 101 days earlier. A number of them were regular buyers in 2025 including customers 117 and 145.

2. **The reason it matters**: It's much easier and less expensive to get a customer to return than to acquire a new one, and customers who have already purchased recently lapse are the easiest to bring back.

3. **Recommendation*8: Launch a Win Back campaign for the customers who have been lapsed for 90-120 days (send a message + Accessory offer). 


# value bands

1. **Finding**: The ten highest spenders (customers 33, 93, 19, 31, 65, 68, 60, 3, 64 and 129) spent KSh 7.19 million between them, 29.8% of all revenue. Customer 33 alone spent KSh 848,119 (3.5% of the total). About 7% of the 150 customers.

2. **Why it matters**: If a couple of the accounts are lost, the revenue is dependent on a small number of accounts (20%) and it will be obvious. The higher spenders require more attention than the average consumer and deserve being given more.

3. **Recommendation**: Each top ten customer, name a contact, provide them first access to new stock, maintain a monthly order date check and call a customer if the order date is slow, to prevent him/her from falling into the lapsed list. Consider these customers the "High Value" (80th percentile and higher) group for value-based marketing.
